### Pg 4/ WHAT I’VE DONE: NON-FICTION SHOWS SCRIPT

Elvis Presley’s Death  
[Listen here](https://skoob.ai/skoob/shows/elvis-presleys-death)

Episode Architecture

Ep1/ King's Last Rock and Roll  
*The rise, the decline, the final hours.*  
The episode establishes Elvis as a cultural phenomenon before tracing his deteriorating health and reconstructing the final night and morning at Graceland. It builds toward the discovery of his body.

Ep2/ Pills, Prescriptions, and Paranoia  
*The medical controversy behind the decline.*  
This episode moves from Elvis's personal dependence on prescription medication to the wider medical culture, the role of his doctors, and questions about responsibility and accountability.

Ep3/ The Lonely King  
*The person behind the public image.*  
This episode explores Elvis’ grief, relationships, spiritual searching, career frustrations, and isolation. It adds emotional context to the first two episodes and sets up the questions that follow his death.

Ep4/ The King Never Died  
*From the official account to the conspiracy theories.*  
The final episode examines stories about the casket, alleged sightings, the John Burrows identity, witness protection, and the moved grave, then considers why these theories endure despite the lack of credible evidence that Elvis survived.

Script Development

The script I create is more than a collection of facts. Once I select a topic, I write the script and then reshape the material into a more deliberate listening experience.

\- The opening hook: Establishing Elvis's final days through the cancelled concert in Portland, his declining health and the unanswered questions surrounding his death.  
\- The narrative sequence: Tracing his journey from his childhood in Tupelo and rise to fame to the pressures of stardom, personal struggles and final hours at Graceland.  
\- The episode architecture: Building the four-part story around Elvis's rise and decline, prescription medication and medical controversy, his personal life and isolation, and the conspiracy theories surrounding his death.  
\- The titles: Using references to Elvis's music, public image and enduring legacy to give each episode its own identity while reflecting its central theme.  
\- The transitions: Moving from the making of a cultural icon to the pressures behind his fame, the deterioration of his health and the circumstances surrounding his death.  
\- The editorial direction: Balancing Elvis's public legacy with his personal struggles, distinguishing the official account of his death from speculation, and building towards the questions that continue to surround his story.

### Pg 5/ WHAT I’VE DONE: NON-FICTION SHOWS SCRIPT

Honeymoon Murder of Anni Dewani  
[Listen here](https://skoob.ai/skoob/shows/honeymoon-murder-of-anni-dewani)

Episode Architecture

Ep1/ The Bride in the Back Seat  
*The honeymoon, the ambush and the woman at the centre of the case.*  
The opening episode introduces Anni's life, her relationship with Shrien and the events leading up to their honeymoon in South Africa. It reconstructs the night of the hijacking and the discovery of Anni's body, establishing the central mystery.

Ep2/ The Perfect Crime  
*An investigation that changes everything.*  
As detectives investigate the apparent carjacking, the evidence begins to challenge the initial account. The taxi driver's confession introduces allegations of a murder-for-hire plot, shifting suspicion towards Anni's husband and triggering an international investigation.

Ep3/ The Man Who Ordered the Hit  
*The allegations, the hidden life, and the trial.*  
The third episode examines the prosecution's case against Shrien Dewani, the delays surrounding his extradition and the personal details brought into the courtroom. As key witnesses face cross-examination, inconsistencies begin to emerge, putting the prosecution's narrative under pressure.

Ep4/ Reasonable Doubt  
*An acquittal, competing theories and unanswered questions.*  
The final episode follows the collapse of the prosecution's case and Shrien Dewani's acquittal. It examines competing explanations of the crime, the reliability of witness testimony and the lasting divide between the legal outcome and the questions that remained.

Script Development

\- The opening hook: Introducing Anni's murder during what should have been a honeymoon, establishing the apparent carjacking and the circumstances that would soon place her husband at the centre of an international investigation.  
\- The narrative sequence: Tracing Anni and Shrien's relationship, their wedding and honeymoon in South Africa before moving into the hijacking, the investigation, the taxi driver's confession and the eventual trial.  
\- The episode architecture: Building the four-part story around Anni's final hours, the investigation and emerging murder-for-hire allegations, the prosecution's case and courtroom proceedings, and the acquittal that left several questions unresolved.  
\- The titles: Drawing on the honeymoon setting, the apparent crime, the allegations against Shrien and the eventual legal outcome to give each episode a distinct identity.  
\- The transitions: Connecting Anni's personal story and the events of the honeymoon to the changing investigation, witness accounts, extradition proceedings and courtroom developments.  
\- The editorial direction: Distinguishing the prosecution's allegations from witness testimony and established courtroom evidence, while examining the inconsistencies, competing explanations and reasonable doubt that shaped the outcome of the case.

**Psy-Fi Drama Original Concept: The Fifth Minute**

*What if the person you keep meeting in your dreams exists somewhere in the real world?*

The Fifth Minute is an original psychological sci-fi concept about lucid dreaming, consciousness, and two people who begin to experience their dream lives in very different ways. What starts as a strange personal discovery gradually becomes a story about escapism, identity, and the lives we choose to inhabit.

My creative process  
The project began with a simple question about lucid dreaming and the strange relationship between sleep, awareness, and time. I developed it into a larger narrative exploring the possibility of shared dream experiences and the consequences of treating dreams as an alternative to reality.

The development process involves building the story's central premise, establishing its internal rules, developing contrasting character arcs, and exploring different directions for its final act.

What I'm exploring  
\- Original premise and world-building  
\- Character psychology and emotional conflict  
\- Narrative structure, reveals, and pacing  
\- Alternative endings and thematic development

### Pg 8/ CONCEPT LAB: FICTION MICRO-DRAMA INDEPENDENT CONCEPT FOR NYKAA

**Fiction Micro Drama Independent Concept for [Nykaa](https://www.nykaa.com/): The Vanity and Everything Else**

*Turns out, getting ready is the easy part.*

The Vanity and Everything Else is an original contemporary Indian female-led dramedy about three women sharing an apartment, each navigating a different stage of life. One is preparing for her first big career break, another is trying to put a breakup behind her, and the third is secretly planning a future that doesn't include the other two.

Their bathroom mirror becomes an unlikely meeting point: where makeup is applied, outfits are debated, pep talks are exchanged, and occasionally, the truth slips out. Between rushed mornings, late-night conversations and the small rituals of getting ready, the three women begin to discover that growing up doesn't always mean having everything figured out.

My creative process  
The project began with the idea of using an everyday space, i.e. a shared bathroom mirror, as the setting for conversations women don't always have elsewhere. The development process involves building three distinct characters with their own relationships with beauty, confidence and identity, and using their getting-ready routines as a natural entry point into their stories. Each episode focuses on a relatable moment or conflict, allowing the characters' friendships to evolve through humour, vulnerability and honest conversations.

Nykaa’s brand integration here is designed to feel organic. Beauty and skincare products become part of the characters' everyday routines and personal rituals, rather than interruptions to the story.

What I'm exploring  
\- Character development and female friendship dynamics  
\- Contemporary Indian urban settings and everyday experiences  
\- Beauty, self-image and personal identity  
\- Episodic storytelling and character-led narratives  
\- Natural dialogue, humour and emotional honesty  
\- Organic brand integration through everyday routines
